<div class="fleuron" aria-hidden="true">❦ ❦ ❦</div>

<div class="poem">
  <span class="line">The life so short, the craft so long to learn,</span>
  <span class="line">The test so hard, so sharp the conquering,</span>
  <span class="line">The dreadful joy, that slips away in turn;</span>
  <span class="line">All this I mean by Love, that my feelings</span>
  <span class="line">Are astonished by his wondrous working</span>
  <span class="line">So deeply indeed, that when I think,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">I know not whether I float or sink.</span>
  <span class="line">For although I know not Love in deed,</span>
  <span class="line">Nor know how he pays folk for their hire,</span>
  <span class="line">Yet full often in my books I read,</span>
  <span class="line">Of his miracles, and his cruel ire,</span>
  <span class="line">There I read he will be Lord and sire;</span>
  <span class="line">I dare not say much, his strokes are so sore,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Just God save such a lord! I&#x27;ll say no more.</span>
  <span class="line">By habit, for pleasure or for lore,</span>
  <span class="line">I often read books, as I have told.</span>
  <span class="line">But why do I say this? Some time before,</span>
  <span class="line">Not long ago, I happened to behold,</span>
  <span class="line">A weighty book, written with letters old;</span>
  <span class="line">And thereupon, a certain thing to learn,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">That long day did I its pages turn.</span>
  <span class="line">For from the oldest fields, as men do say,</span>
  <span class="line">Comes forth all this new corn from year to year;</span>
  <span class="line">And from the oldest writings, in good faith,</span>
  <span class="line">Comes forth this new knowledge that men can hear.</span>
  <span class="line">But now to my purpose in this matter—</span>
  <span class="line">To read on so began me to beguile,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">That all the day seemed but a little while.</span>
  <span class="line">This book of which I now make mention,</span>
  <span class="line">Was entitled thus, as I shall tell:</span>
  <span class="line">&quot;Cicero on the Dream of Scipio.&quot;</span>
  <span class="line">Seven chapters it had, of heaven and hell,</span>
  <span class="line">And the earth, and the souls that therein dwell,</span>
  <span class="line">Of which topic, as briefly as I can,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">I shall relate the meaning and the plan.</span>
  <span class="line">First it tells of when Scipio had come</span>
  <span class="line">To Africa; Massinissa, at the sight,</span>
  <span class="line">Clasped him in arms, by joy overcome.</span>
  <span class="line">Then it tells their talk, and all the delight</span>
  <span class="line">That came between them, until came the night;</span>
  <span class="line">And how his ancestor, Africanus dear,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Appeared before him in sleep, strange and near.</span>
  <span class="line">From his starry place, Africanus showed</span>
  <span class="line">Him Carthage, all laid out in bright array,</span>
  <span class="line">And warned of his fortune, yet bestowed,</span>
  <span class="line">And said: whatever man, learned or lay,</span>
  <span class="line">Who loves the common good, and speeds its way,</span>
  <span class="line">He shall unto a blissful place ascend,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Where there is joy that lasts without an end.</span>
  <span class="line">Then Scipio asked if those who died here</span>
  <span class="line">Have life and dwelling in another place;</span>
  <span class="line">And Africanus said, &quot;Yes, have no fear;</span>
  <span class="line">On this present world, our life&#x27;s narrow space</span>
  <span class="line">Is but a kind of death, whose path we trace,</span>
  <span class="line">And righteous folk shall go, after they die,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">To heaven.&quot; And showed him the galaxy.</span>
  <span class="line">He showed him the little earth that lies here,</span>
  <span class="line">So small besides the heavens&#x27; quantity;</span>
  <span class="line">And after that he showed him the nine spheres,</span>
  <span class="line">And Scipio then heard the melody</span>
  <span class="line">That comes forth from those spheres three times three,</span>
  <span class="line">The source of all music and melody</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">On earth below, and cause of harmony.</span>
  <span class="line">Then he warned him, since Earth is so slight</span>
  <span class="line">So full of torment and so harsh in grace,</span>
  <span class="line">That in the world he should find no delight.</span>
  <span class="line">And Africanus said, in some years&#x27; space</span>
  <span class="line">That every star should come into its place</span>
  <span class="line">To where it first begun; and out of mind</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Shall pass all worldly things wrought by mankind.</span>
  <span class="line">Then Scipio begged him: show me my role,</span>
  <span class="line">Teach me my path to that heavenly sphere;</span>
  <span class="line">And he said, &quot;First, know your soul is deathless.</span>
  <span class="line">Look diligently that you work and steer</span>
  <span class="line">To the common good; then you shall not miss</span>
  <span class="line">To follow swiftly to that place so dear,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">That is suffused with bliss and with souls clear.</span>
  <span class="line">But law-breakers, he went on to explain,</span>
  <span class="line">And lecherous folk, when their lives had worn out,</span>
  <span class="line">Shall reel around the earth in constant pain,</span>
  <span class="line">Until years have passed, without a doubt,</span>
  <span class="line">And then, forgiven for their grubby rout,</span>
  <span class="line">They shall all come unto that blissful place,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">To which may God send you with all his grace!</span>
  <span class="line">With the day failing, as the gloomy night</span>
  <span class="line">Which robs all beasts of their business,</span>
  <span class="line">Bereft me of my book for want of light,</span>
  <span class="line">So for my bed I began to undress,</span>
  <span class="line">Filled with thoughts and restless heaviness;</span>
  <span class="line">For I had both that which I did not want,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">And lacked the thing that I had wanted most.</span>
  <span class="line">But finally my spirit, at the last,</span>
  <span class="line">Full weary from the labours of the day,</span>
  <span class="line">Took rest and stuck me in a sleep so fast,</span>
  <span class="line">That in my sleep I dreamed it, as I lay,</span>
  <span class="line">How Africanus, in that same array</span>
  <span class="line">That Scipio had seen him wear before,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Had come to me, and stood right at my door.</span>
  <span class="line">The weary hunter sleeping in his bed,</span>
  <span class="line">In dreams returns to the woods that he knows;</span>
  <span class="line">The judge dreams how his cases will be sped;</span>
  <span class="line">The carter dreams of how his cart still goes;</span>
  <span class="line">The rich dream of gold; the knight fights his foes;</span>
  <span class="line">The sick man dreams he drinks of the cask;</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">The lover dreams his lady&#x27;s won at last.</span>
  <span class="line">I cannot guess what the cause was, unless</span>
  <span class="line">What I read of Africanus before</span>
  <span class="line">Made me dream of his terrible largesse,</span>
  <span class="line">But thus said he: &quot;You have so well sought for</span>
  <span class="line">Lessons to learn from my decrepit lore,</span>
  <span class="line">Which Macrobius thought had much to say,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">That some of your labor would I repay!&quot;</span>
  <span class="line">O Cytherea! Blissful lady sweet,</span>
  <span class="line">Whose firebrand tames all hearts as you desire,</span>
  <span class="line">And who first made me this vision to meet,</span>
  <span class="line">Be now my help in this, as I require;</span>
  <span class="line">As sure as I saw you, your hanged fire,</span>
  <span class="line">North-northwest, when first I began to write,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">So give me might to rhyme and to endite!</span>
  <span class="line">With this, Africanus seized my hand,</span>
  <span class="line">And forth with him to a gate I was brought,</span>
  <span class="line">Enclosing a park, walled with green stone grand;</span>
  <span class="line">And over the gate, with letters huge wrought,</span>
  <span class="line">There were two verses written, as I had thought,</span>
  <span class="line">On either side, of fateful difference,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Of which I shall tell the significance.</span>
  <span class="line">&quot;Through me, men go into that blissful place</span>
  <span class="line">Of heart&#x27;s healing and deadly wounds&#x27; cure;</span>
  <span class="line">Through me men go unto the well of Grace,</span>
  <span class="line">Where green and lusty May shall ever endure;</span>
  <span class="line">This is the way to all good adventure;</span>
  <span class="line">Cast off your sorrow, let your spirits lift,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">I am open. Pass in, and go thee swift!&quot;</span>
  <span class="line">&quot;Through me men go,&quot; then spoke that other side,</span>
  <span class="line">&quot;Unto the mortal strokes of the barbed spear,</span>
  <span class="line">Of which Disdain and Rebuff is the guide,</span>
  <span class="line">Where trees shall bear no fruit, nor leaves appear.</span>
  <span class="line">This stream leads you to the sorrowful weir,</span>
  <span class="line">Where fish are caught in drought and left to die;</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Avoidance is the only remedy.&quot;</span>
  <span class="line">These shining verses of gold and black I read,</span>
  <span class="line">And transfixed stood amazedly behold,</span>
  <span class="line">For with one I was gripped with fearful dread,</span>
  <span class="line">And with the other verse my heart grew bold;</span>
  <span class="line">One warmed me, the other chilled me cold,</span>
  <span class="line">I had no wit, for doubting, how to choose</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">To enter or flee, save myself or lose.</span>
  <span class="line">Just as between two lodestones placed just so,</span>
  <span class="line">Of even strength, a piece of iron set</span>
  <span class="line">Will have no power to move to nor fro—</span>
  <span class="line">For what the one may pull, the other lets—</span>
  <span class="line">Was I; unsure whether it was better yet</span>
  <span class="line">To stay or go, till Africanus my guide</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Seized me, and shoved me in at the gates wide,</span>
  <span class="line">And said, &quot;It stands written plain in your face,</span>
  <span class="line">Your error, though you won&#x27;t tell it to me;</span>
  <span class="line">But do not fear to come into this place,</span>
  <span class="line">For this writing is nothing meant by thee,</span>
  <span class="line">Nor by any, less he Love&#x27;s servant be;</span>
  <span class="line">And you have lost your taste of love, I guess,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">As a sick man has of sweet and bitterness.</span>
  <span class="line">But nonetheless, though your spirit is dull,</span>
  <span class="line">That which you cannot do, you may yet see;</span>
  <span class="line">For many a man who can&#x27;t bear the pull,</span>
  <span class="line">Loves to watch the wrestling - don&#x27;t you agree? -</span>
  <span class="line">And finds whether he does better, or he;</span>
  <span class="line">And if you have a little skill to write,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">I shall now show you something worth the sight!&quot;</span>
  <span class="line">With that, he took my hand without delay,</span>
  <span class="line">From which I took comfort, and went in fast;</span>
  <span class="line">And, Lord, I was more glad that I can say!</span>
  <span class="line">For everywhere that I my eyes did cast,</span>
  <span class="line">Were trees clad in leaves that ever shall last,</span>
  <span class="line">Each in its kind, of colour fresh and green</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">As emerald, that joy it was to see.</span>
  <span class="line">The builder&#x27;s oak, and then the hardy ash;</span>
  <span class="line">The pillar elm, coffin-wood for carrion;</span>
  <span class="line">The piper&#x27;s boxwood; the holly for lash;</span>
  <span class="line">The sailing fir; the cypress, grief&#x27;s companion;</span>
  <span class="line">The shooter yew, the asp for arrows&#x27; shafts;</span>
  <span class="line">The olive of peace, and the drinker&#x27;s vine,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">The victor&#x27;s palm; the laurel, fortune&#x27;s sign.</span>
  <span class="line">A garden I saw, with blossomy boughs,</span>
  <span class="line">Beside a river, in a green meadow,</span>
  <span class="line">Where sweetness dwells forever, and endows</span>
  <span class="line">The field with flowers — white, blue, and yellow —</span>
  <span class="line">And cool well-streams, nothing dead, in quick flow,</span>
  <span class="line">That swam full of small fishes, darting light,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">With fins of red and their scales silver-bright.</span>
  <span class="line">On every bough I heard the birds there sing,</span>
  <span class="line">With angel-voices in their harmony,</span>
  <span class="line">Some busied themselves with their chicks&#x27; upbringing;</span>
  <span class="line">The rabbits hurried to their play nearby.</span>
  <span class="line">And further all about I began to spy</span>
  <span class="line">The timid roe, the buck, the hart and hind,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">And squirrels, and small beasts of gentle kind.</span>
  <span class="line">Of instruments, each one stringed in accord</span>
  <span class="line">I heard playing a ravishing sweetness,</span>
  <span class="line">That God, who maker is of all and lord,</span>
  <span class="line">Never heard music better, as I guess;</span>
  <span class="line">With that a wind, scarcely could it be less,</span>
  <span class="line">Made in the green leaves a murmuring sound</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">In concord with the birdsong all around.</span>
  <span class="line">The air of that place so temperate was,</span>
  <span class="line">That never was grievance of heat or cold;</span>
  <span class="line">There grew also every wholesome spice and grass,</span>
  <span class="line">And there no man would grew sick nor grow old;</span>
  <span class="line">And yet joy waxed there a thousandfold</span>
  <span class="line">beyond all telling. Never came the night,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">But always clear day, to every man&#x27;s sight.</span>
  <span class="line">Under a tree, beside a well, I saw</span>
  <span class="line">Cupid, our lord, his arrows forge and file,</span>
  <span class="line">And at his feet his bow ready to draw;</span>
  <span class="line">Desire, his daughter, tempered all the while</span>
  <span class="line">The arrows in the spring, and with her guile</span>
  <span class="line">She set them each to their appointed end,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Some to slay, and some to wound and to rend.</span>
  <span class="line">Then I at once was aware of Pleasure,</span>
  <span class="line">And Finery, and Want, and Courtesy,</span>
  <span class="line">And of Craft that can and has the measure</span>
  <span class="line">To force a fool creature into folly—</span>
  <span class="line">Disfigured was she, here I shall not lie;</span>
  <span class="line">And by himself, under an oak, I guess,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">I saw Delight, who stood with Gentleness.</span>
  <span class="line">I saw Beauty, bare of any attire,</span>
  <span class="line">And Youth, all full of sport and jollity,</span>
  <span class="line">Foolhardiness, Flattery, and Desire,</span>
  <span class="line">Messenger, Bribery, and other three—</span>
  <span class="line">Their names shall not here be told by me—</span>
  <span class="line">And upon jasper pillars, huge and long</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">I saw a temple of brass, firm and strong.</span>
  <span class="line">About that temple, dancing there daily</span>
  <span class="line">Were troops of women, gorgeous and austere,</span>
  <span class="line">Some fair by nature, and some dressed gaily;</span>
  <span class="line">Loose-haired and disheveled danced they there—</span>
  <span class="line">That was their office always, year by year—</span>
  <span class="line">And on the temple, of doves white and fair</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">I saw roosting, many a hundred pair.</span>
  <span class="line">Before the temple very solemnly</span>
  <span class="line">Lady Peace sat, a curtain in her hand:</span>
  <span class="line">And beside, wonderfully discreetly,</span>
  <span class="line">Dame Patience sitting next to her I found</span>
  <span class="line">With pale face, perched upon a hill of sand;</span>
  <span class="line">And nearest, within and also without,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Promise and Art, and of their folk a crowd.</span>
  <span class="line">Within the temple, sighs as hot as fire</span>
  <span class="line">I heard murmur, hurry, and swirl about;</span>
  <span class="line">Those sighs so engendered with deep desire,</span>
  <span class="line">They set every altar to burning out</span>
  <span class="line">With darts of new flames; and I had no doubt</span>
  <span class="line">That all the keen sorrows which I could see</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Sprung from their bitter goddess Jealousy.</span>
  <span class="line">The god Priapus saw I, as I went,</span>
  <span class="line">Within the temple, in sovereign place,</span>
  <span class="line">Arrayed as he was in that event</span>
  <span class="line">When the ass brayed loud and spoiled his chase,</span>
  <span class="line">His staff held in his hand, and on his face</span>
  <span class="line">A look of stalled intent; and all around</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Folk heaped fresh garlands on his gaudy crown.</span>
  <span class="line">And in a secret corner, in disport,</span>
  <span class="line">I found Venus and her porter, Richness,</span>
  <span class="line">A noble dame, of mannered, haughty sort;</span>
  <span class="line">That place was dark, but further on lightness</span>
  <span class="line">I saw a light, scarcely could it be less,</span>
  <span class="line">Where on a bed of gold she lay to rest,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">Until the hot sun sunk into the west.</span>
  <span class="line">Her gilded hair was banded with a thread</span>
  <span class="line">Of gold, and all unbraided as she lay,</span>
  <span class="line">And naked from the breast unto the head</span>
  <span class="line">Men might behold her; and, truly to say,</span>
  <span class="line">The rest was vestured in a pleasing way,</span>
  <span class="line">With a subtle kerchief, Valence-thin —</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">No stronger cloth to shield her gentle skin.</span>
  <span class="line">The place was thick with a thousand scents sweet,</span>
  <span class="line">And Bacchus, god of wine, sat by her side,</span>
  <span class="line">And Ceres next, who relieves hunger&#x27;s heat;</span>
  <span class="line">And in the midst lay Venus in her pride,</span>
  <span class="line">To whom two young folk knelt, and, kneeling, cried</span>
  <span class="line">For her to help; but thus I let her lie,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">And deeper in the temple I cast my eye.</span>
  <span class="line">And there, in spite of Diana the chaste,</span>
  <span class="line">Many a broken bow hung on the wall</span>
  <span class="line">Of maidens, whose long service went to waste;</span>
  <span class="line">And painted all over throughout that hall</span>
  <span class="line">Were many a tale, of which I can recall</span>
  <span class="line">But few: Callisto, and Atalanta&#x27;s state,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">And other maidens of forgotten fate.</span>
  <span class="line">Semiramis, Candace, and Hercules,</span>
  <span class="line">Biblis, Dido, Thisbee and Pyramus,</span>
  <span class="line">Tristram, Isolde, Paris, and Achilles,</span>
  <span class="line">Helen, Cleopatra, and Troilus,</span>
  <span class="line">Scylla, and she who mothered Romulus—</span>
  <span class="line">All these were painted on that other side,</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">And all their love, and how for love they died.</span>
  <span class="line">When I returned again to that sweet place,</span>
  <span class="line">Of which I spoke, so gentle and so green,</span>
  <span class="line">I walked on, to bring me some solace.</span>
  <span class="line">And then I saw her sitting there: a queen</span>
  <span class="line">Whose brilliance, as the sun in summer sheen</span>
  <span class="line">Surpasses stars, was so beyond all measure;</span>
  <span class="line stanza-end">She was fairer than any creature.</span>
  <span class="line">In that glade, upon a hill of flowers,</span>
  <span class="line">Was set this noble goddess of Nature;</span>
